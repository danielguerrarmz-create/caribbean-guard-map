# Precedent catalog — Latin American nonprofits protecting people and marine life

Compiled 2026-08-06 from two parallel verification sweeps: **family A** (protecting
people in and around the water) and **family B** (protecting marine life and coastal
environments). 29 organizations verified by direct fetch. Failures are listed rather
than dropped.

**Fit score** is closeness to Caribbean Guard's profile, weighted toward: small
volunteer org, Spanish-first, tourist plus local audience, safety information as the
core product, coastal. 5 is closest.

**Verification rule applied throughout:** nothing is listed that was not fetched. Two
organizations that blocked automated fetch are recorded in the failures table rather
than described from search snippets. The one exception, FECOGU, was opened in a real
browser instead and is documented in `04-sector-scan.md` section 1b.

---

## The headline finding

**Caribbean Guard is a founding member of the Federación Costarricense de Guardavidas**,
constituted 2024-10-08 alongside **Swim Safe** and **Playa Grande Lifeguards**
(source: delfino.cr, corroborated by FECOGU's own affiliates page).

That reframes this whole catalog. The two most relevant peers are not arm's-length
precedents to study, they are **siblings inside Caribbean Guard's own federation**.
See `04-sector-scan.md` §1b for what the federation's site actually looks like, which
is the strategic finding of the entire research wave.

## The five closest, in detail

### 1. Guardavidas Costa Ballena — Costa Rica, Pacific — fit 5

The single closest structural analogue found. Same country, same function, same
volunteer model, same funding precarity.

Their own framing, verbatim from the site:

> "Every day, lives are at risk on Costa Ballena beaches. Our lifeguards prevent
> drownings, but without funding, they can't stay on the beach."

Logbook data spanning April to December 2025: **6,700+ preventions, 17 rescues, zero
on-duty drownings.** Two beaches, one fully funded (Playa Dominical) and one still
fundraising (Playa Hermosa). International volunteer lifeguards plus a local Junior
Lifeguard Program.

Built on **Astro**, WebP and AVIF, no framework bloat, credited to Hermosa Software.
Notable because it proves the fast undecorated approach is also the credible one in
this category. Copyright renders as "© 2026", which appears to be a template artifact
rather than a real date.

**Relationship to Fundación SOMOS.** The sector scan separately found SOMOS running a
"Costa Ballena Lifeguards" programme at es.somos.cr with a UNDP and US Embassy funding
pathway. These are two web presences around the same stretch of coast. **We have not
verified how the two entities relate.** Do not state a relationship in any copy until
someone confirms it.

### 2. Santa Teresa Lifeguards — Costa Rica, Nicoya — fit 5

Second volunteer lifeguard org, Pacific side, and the one that most resembles what
Caribbean Guard's site could be.

2025 totals published on the site: **216 rescues, 6,628 prevention interventions, 131
first-aid responses, 365 patrol days.** Recruits certified lifeguards internationally
as volunteers. Bilingual EN/ES for a split local-and-international audience, which is
exactly Caribbean Guard's situation.

Uses one educational rip-current diagram **alongside** real photography rather than
instead of it. Webflow. Footer copyright reads 2023 while the latest blog post is
April 2026, so the footer is stale even here.

### 3. ANAI — Costa Rica, Gandoca-Manzanillo — fit 5

**Direct neighbour.** Runs the sea turtle programme at Gandoca-Manzanillo since 1985,
the same refuge Caribbean Guard's coastline touches. References 50 years of work and
partnerships across more than 80 communities.

Site is English only with no language switcher, and the copyright reads 2020. Included
at fit 5 for adjacency and institutional weight, **not** as a design precedent. If
Caribbean Guard ever needs a local conservation partner with deep roots, this is it.

### 4. Turtle Rescue Cahuita — Costa Rica, Cahuita — fit 5

**Direct neighbour.** Monitors roughly 53 km of Caribbean beach across two programmes
(Playa Grande 6 km, Moín 16 km). Legally registered nonprofit founded 2015, lean team,
volunteer-supplemented.

Bilingual EN/ES with a footer toggle. WordPress. Footer reads 2025 and news items run
into 2026, so it is actively maintained, unlike most of this list. The closest thing
to a well-kept small bilingual nonprofit site on this coast.

### 5. COASTS — Costa Rica, Gandoca — fit 5

**Direct neighbour**, based in Gandoca, Sixaola, Talamanca, Limón. Took over the
Gandoca turtle monitoring project from ANAI in 2011.

The most useful thing here is **how they establish legitimacy without an annual
report**: they publish concrete impact statistics (7,695 hours patrolled, 49,371
hatchlings released, 276 females protected, 7.2 metric tons of plastic removed,
$158,223 contributed to the local economy) alongside their **cédula jurídica
#3-002-689632** and SDG-alignment badges. No financial transparency documents.

That combination, a registration number plus specific self-reported numbers, is a
cheap and copyable credibility pattern for an organization Caribbean Guard's size.

## Well executed, worth close study

- **Guardavidas Costa Ballena** — modern static stack, honest funding-gap messaging,
  numbers where the risk statement lands.
- **SOBRASA (Brazil)** — not a scale match and not Spanish, but the strongest
  prevention messaging in the set. Slogan "Prevenir é Salvar, Educar para não se
  Afogar", statistic-forward homepage ("Um brasileiro morre afogado a cada 90
  minutos"), dedicated volunteer subdomain. Study for messaging, not structure.
- **ICT Guardavidas Autorizados** — the actual regulatory context Caribbean Guard
  operates inside, under Law No. 9790, listing seven authorized lifeguard
  associations plus municipal contacts across Guanacaste, Puntarenas and **Limón**.
  Genuinely bilingual. This is the authoritative source for who else is on this list.
- **Sea Turtle Conservancy, "Find My Turtle"** — a clean public-facing live tracking
  map. Different use case, but a good model for presenting live status to a general
  audience.
- **Projeto Tamar** — real-time leatherback telemetry presented to the public.

## Cautionary examples

- **Guardavidas SEAL Chile** — a listed ILS full member whose public site shows no
  activity since October 2018. Default WordPress.com theme, no custom domain.
- **FMSRA (Mexico)** — the ILS full member for the largest country in this list,
  reduced to a single Blogger post from August 2014. Formal membership and a
  functioning public presence are unrelated.
- **WIDECAST** — copyright footer reads 2021 despite the organization apparently
  remaining active. Letting dated markers go stale erodes trust more than having none.
- **IMN Costa Rica** — "Page updated: October 31th, 2023" sitting over live map data.
  A stale footer timestamp is worse than no timestamp, because it actively misleads.
- **latinamericanseaturtles.org** — a former sea turtle nonprofit domain now squatted
  and serving gambling content. Flagged separately to AJ as a domain-hygiene warning.
- **Reef Check** — a `reefcheck.us` subdomain currently 302s visitors to advertising
  and spam domains, with a tracking cookie scoped to the parent domain expiring in
  2094. What kills a small nonprofit's web presence is usually a DNS record nobody
  owns after the person who set it up leaves.

## What this sector looks like online, honestly

Three patterns held across all 29 organizations.

**1. Formal standing and a working website are unrelated.** Mexico's national ILS
member is an eleven-year-old Blogger post. Chile's has not been touched since 2018.
Two of the three FECOGU founding members have no website at all. Caribbean Guard is
already, by a wide margin, in the better half of this field, and the rebuilt site puts
it near the top.

**2. Nobody in the people-safety family has a map.** Not one of the fourteen
lifeguard, rescue or water-safety organizations in this catalog publishes an
interactive hazard map. Every map in the table belongs to a marine-life org, and they
are all tracking maps for animals, not safety maps for people.

**3. The credibility currency is numbers, not design.** Costa Ballena leads with 6,700
preventions and zero on-duty drownings. Santa Teresa with 216 rescues across 365
patrol days. COASTS with 7,695 hours patrolled and a cédula jurídica. Every
well-regarded org in this set answers "why should I trust you" with specific
self-reported figures, not with visual polish.

Caribbean Guard has these numbers. The sector scan surfaced some of them from press
coverage. They are not on the homepage yet, and **they should come from Caribbean
Guard directly rather than from a news article**, per the standing rule against
publishing what we cannot source to the organization itself.
